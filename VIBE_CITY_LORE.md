# Vibe City Lore

**Document type:** Vibe City Design Bible companion  
**Status:** Scope reserved for future world-identity planning  
**Authority:** Subordinate to `VIBE_CITY_MASTER_DESIGN.md`, Version 0

---

## Purpose

This document is reserved for Vibe City's branding, terminology, visual language, architectural character, and atmosphere. It should help future contributors create a world that feels coherent without introducing decorative systems that misrepresent the underlying platform.

## Future Planning Topics

Future revisions should define:

- Vibe City branding principles;
- naming conventions for buildings, floors, suites, departments, rooms, agents, services, and Credits;
- approved product and world terminology;
- architectural style of the initial high-rise;
- identity of STG administrative floors versus tenant floors;
- signage and digital-plaque conventions;
- office aesthetics and customization boundaries;
- materials, lighting, color, and environmental tone;
- visual expressions of active, idle, offline, blocked, suspended, and unavailable states;
- the atmosphere of private, shared, public, and restricted spaces;
- how future districts and buildings retain a common Vibe City identity; and
- accessibility requirements for identity, signage, and status communication.

## Current Baseline Conclusions

- The first Vibe City environment is one high-rise.
- The high-rise represents the hosted platform or infrastructure environment.
- The world should feel like a real headquarters for each customer rather than a decorative game layer.
- Decorative objects are allowed for atmosphere, orientation, architectural credibility, and comfort.
- Interactive or meaningful objects should normally map to a real platform object, system, resource, state, or permission.
- Decoration must not imply nonexistent workers, work, access, service capacity, or platform capability.
- Room signage and digital plaques should identify real tenants, departments, agents, or services.
- Dark workstations, locked offices, badges, and access points should communicate real service or permission states rather than arbitrary set dressing.
