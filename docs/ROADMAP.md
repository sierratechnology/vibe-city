# Prioritized roadmap

## September 24 shared-survival release
Implemented: stable backpack grid, belt/item actions and resource drops; recipe search/categories/counts and a browser-local pin; atomic bulk cargo transfers; owned-bunk recovery with safe fallback; saved optional expedition milestones; original tool/footstep/landing cues, work poses and particle feedback. Local simulation uses elapsed-time substeps and server snapshots avoid redundant revalidation of already-validated baselines. See docs/research/survival-loop-2026-09-24.md and wiki/survival-loop.html.

Still required before calling this a 50-human production launch: a sustained real-internet playtest on the actual hosting/Redis plan, reconnect under load, physical controllers/phones, and lower-end device measurements. Local simulated load and one rendered crowd are evidence, not a substitute for those checks.

## 0 — Finish evaluating First Signal (current slice)
- Two humans play together on separate LAN machines. Evaluate camera feel, crafting clarity, construction reach and shelter readability.
- Test Safari, Firefox and lower-powered hardware. Measure sustained frame times, network latency, reconnect and server sleep/wake.
- Tune materials, camera obstruction, nameplates, sound and resource feedback based on playtesting.
- Preserve the small reproducible loop as a regression target.

## 1 — A dependable shared settlement
- Password recovery, character deletion, administrative controls, and an explicit migration path for legacy anonymous pilots. Accounts, three-character selection, reconnect and daily world backups are implemented.
- Input sequence acknowledgment and reconciliation, delta snapshots, latency/loss tests, build ownership permissions and collaborative dismantling.
- Richer modular snapping: passable doorway frames, directional angled canopies, nearby-operated sealed doors, and low-rise Deck stairs are implemented. A Deck stair rotates among four supported free deck edges and provides a traversable terrain-to-current-deck transition only. The bounded sealed door blocks and seals its edge while closed, becomes traversable and unsealed while open, and persists its server-owned state. These pieces do not add locks, automatic doors, pressure equalization, airlock cycles, room pressurization, structural support, multi-level building, ladders, elevators, arbitrary vertical building, or generalized enclosure simulation; those broader systems remain planned.
- One buildable Workbench and its bounded Field meal queue are implemented: one active job, two pending jobs, five seconds of server-running game time, exact-once delivery, blocked-output preservation, and no offline catch-up. A nearby owner can grant or revoke queue use for individual saved collaborators; every accepted job reserves that actor's ingredients and returns output only to that actor, without granting ownership or dismantle/cargo authority. Each actor can cancel only their own pending Field meal for an exact refund, or move it exactly one pending position later when another pending meal follows. The nearby builder can also cancel the earliest collaborator-owned pending meal, including for an offline saved character, with an exact refund to that original character; active, blocked-output and owner-owned jobs remain uncancellable. The Workbench now has one original bounded authored fabrication-station mesh with a generation-safe fail-closed primitive fallback. This is one visual tracer, not a complete art pass, broad asset pipeline, physical-device validation or gameplay expansion. Shared cargo lockers, item transfers, PIN protection, and owner-managed full-transfer grants for individual saved collaborators are implemented. Broader recipes, arbitrary queue reordering, moving jobs earlier, workbench bulk controls, storage or workbench roles, groups, organizations, offline invitations and automation remain planned.
- Accessible remapping and touch/controller controls are implemented. Short generated Interface and World/Gameplay cues now have independent browser-local volume settings and a master mute; audio is gesture-gated and supplementary, with no speech, music, account sync or server/world-save fields. Physical phone speaker/headphone and physical-controller audio remain unverified.

## 2 — A planet worth exploring
- A first deterministic visual biome tracer is implemented: the protected starter region remains Quiet Basin, while seeded distant terrain can classify as Coral Shelf with a distinct palette and accessible text label. Server-authoritative per-character discovery records Quiet Basin and records Coral Shelf once after physical entry; the first accepted Coral Shelf position is also retained as one private, immutable Terminal Map waypoint with readable coordinates. After contact, Terminal → Map provides that character with whole-meter distance and compass-bearing return guidance plus a subordinate clipped visual cue from the current accepted position. These bounded allowlisted records and deterministic guidance survive saves and reconnects without appearing in other characters or public player summaries. Coral Shelf also has a first bounded decorative environment tracer: deterministic, nearby-loaded low-poly fan formations that remain scenery only and stay separate from Coral Crown and Coral Flux identities. This is not navigation automation, fast travel, a complete biome, quest, achievement, telemetry, authored-asset pipeline, hazard, creature, resource, interaction, collision system, or full planet; complete regions and broader streaming work remain planned.
- A first bounded sustainable-farming feedback and ownership slice is implemented for the existing Hydroponic bed: nearby explorers see an accepted server-time growth countdown, Harvest appears only when the accepted due is reached, and the builder can grant or revoke planting and harvesting access for individual saved collaborators. The owner always retains access; an authorized collaborator pays their own water + fiber and receives their own fiber + food harvest. This is not complete farming, offline growth, automation, crop choice, ownership transfer, groups, organizations or physical-device verification; broader tools, equipment progression, sustainable gathering/farming and optional threats remain planned.
- The broken signal ring now has the first original optimized authored asset: a checked-in, budgeted low-poly mesh using weathered ivory, dark blue-green metal, mint energy and restrained coral-lavender accents. A closed local decoder and generation-safe loader retain the primitive fallback on unavailable, stale, malformed or oversized data without changing ruin collision, console access, progression or accessible text. This is one visible asset tracer, not a broad asset pipeline or complete art pass; further original art-language work remains planned.
- The finite construction palette now includes one Deck railing: a low, open two-rail barrier on a supported Deck edge. It shares authoritative edge occupancy, collision, ownership, persistence and dismantling behavior with existing construction while remaining outside shelter, wall-light support and room-sealing semantics. This is not structural support, weather protection, a lock, a complete safety system, an enclosure system or terrain editing.
- The finite construction palette now also includes one freestanding low-tech Camp gate: it rotates among four ground edges without requiring a Deck, shares authoritative edge occupancy, costs exactly 3 ferrite plus 1 fiber, and supports server-authoritative nearby operation with accessible Open/Close meaning. Closed gates block players and creatures; open gates allow traversal. Persistence, ownership, dismantling and exact refunds use existing construction paths. One bounded original authored frame-and-leaf presentation is released with an independent complete primitive fallback; this visual slice is not a complete art-language milestone. This adds no shelter, room sealing, pressure or life support, lock, PIN, role, permission, remote operation, automation, progression, structural support, weather protection, terrain editing or complete safety system.
- Continue expanding the finite construction palette before deciding whether true voxels/terrain editing are essential.

## 3 — Production and inhabited places
- Power, production chains and resource logistics.
- Settler jobs; buildable robots and drones with understandable tasks and costs.
- Deterministic, bounded background production and explicit offline catch-up. No unbounded per-frame simulation while nobody is present.

## 4 — From ground to orbit
- Construct and test spacecraft, then travel to moons and other planets within the server's solar system.
- Colonies and space stations, with server-owned inventories and travel transitions.
- Define world sizes, server budgets and simulation fidelity from measurements.

## 5 — Connected worlds
- Expand moderation, recovery, hosting capacity and abuse defenses beyond the current authenticated public playtest.
- Decide whether interstellar travel connects servers, creates instances or remains within larger server domains.
- Reassess engine/native packaging and cross-platform requirements using evidence from actual players.

This sequence is a dependency order, not a calendar or a claim that future features are already built.
